What is TDEE?

TDEE (Total Daily Energy Expenditure) is the total number of calories your body burns in a day. It includes your basal metabolic rate (BMR), the thermic effect of food (TEF), and all physical activity.

Your TDEE is your maintenance calorie level: eat exactly your TDEE and your weight stays the same. Eat more and you gain. Eat less and you lose. That's the foundation of every nutrition plan.

The Mifflin-St Jeor formula

We use the Mifflin-St Jeor formula (1990), the most accurate and widely recommended formula for BMR calculation. Studies show it is about 10% more accurate than the older Harris-Benedict formula.

Men
BMR = 10 x weight (kg) + 6.25 x height (cm) - 5 x age (years) + 5
Example: 80 kg, 178 cm, 30 years = 10x80 + 6.25x178 - 5x30 + 5 = 1,787.5 kcal/day
Women
BMR = 10 x weight (kg) + 6.25 x height (cm) - 5 x age (years) - 161
Example: 65 kg, 165 cm, 28 years = 10x65 + 6.25x165 - 5x28 - 161 = 1,470 kcal/day

TDEE = BMR x activity multiplier. The multipliers are based on the Harris-Benedict activity scale from 1919 - still the most practical in use today.

Activity levels explained

  • Sedentary (x1.20): Desk job, no regular exercise. Most people underestimate how sedentary they actually are.
  • Lightly active (x1.375): Light exercise or sport 1-3 days per week. Walking, casual cycling.
  • Moderately active (x1.55): Exercise 3-5 days per week. The most accurate level for most strength trainers.
  • Very active (x1.725): Hard training 6-7 days per week. Competitive athletes.
  • Extremely active (x1.90): Physical job plus daily training. Rarely applies.

Rule of thumb: Most people overestimate their activity level. Start with "moderately active", track your weight for 4 weeks, and adjust based on the actual change.

Using your TDEE in practice

Weight loss

A deficit of 300-500 kcal/day is optimal for sustainable fat loss without muscle loss. This gives about 0.3-0.5 kg/week of weight loss.

Muscle gain

A surplus of 200-400 kcal/day supports muscle growth without excessive fat gain. Aim for 1.6-2.2 g/kg bodyweight of protein daily.

Maintenance

Frequently asked questions

How accurate is the TDEE calculator?

TDEE formulas have an accuracy of about 10-15%. They are accurate enough as a starting point. The only way to find your exact TDEE is empirically: track calories for 4 weeks and observe whether your weight rises, falls or stays stable.

Should I recalculate my TDEE?

Yes - after losing more than 5 kg, changing your activity level, or every 3 months as a rule of thumb. Gainfully recalculates automatically when you log your weight.

Does strength training count toward my activity level?

Yes. Strength training 3-4 times per week typically corresponds to "moderately active". The direct calorie burn during lifting is lower than many expect (200-400 kcal per session), but EPOC (afterburn) and increased muscle mass raise your daily BMR.
